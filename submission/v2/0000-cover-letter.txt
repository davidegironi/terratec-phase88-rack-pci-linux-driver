Subject: [PATCH v2 0/2] ALSA: ice1712: support the TerraTec PHASE 88

The TerraTec PHASE 88 uses different front-module hardware from the
EWS88MT even though the ICE1712 driver identifies it as an EWS88MT variant.
The PHASE 88's write-only expander does not respond at the EWS88MT
addresses, and its EEPROM advertises a consumer AC'97 codec and one MIDI
UART that are not representative of the board.

Changes since v1, following Takashi Iwai's review:

  - Add an early, card-specific EEPROM initialization callback and move the
    existing DMX6Fire fixup out of generic chip initialization.
  - Move the PHASE 88 AC'97 and second-MIDI UART EEPROM fixups into the EWS
    driver callback, which runs before generic hardware setup.
  - Keep a NULL guard in consumer capture open as a defensive fallback for
    other boards whose EEPROM advertises AC'97 but whose codec fails to init.
    The PHASE 88 EEPROM fixup prevents its nonexistent consumer PCM from being
    created in the first place.
  - Use snd_ctl_boolean_mono_info for the Line/Mic boolean control and fix
    the PHASE 88 initialization indentation.
  - Give TS88 its own model string, and update the PHASE 88 expander shadow
    only after a successful write so an I2C error cannot corrupt it.
  - Tighten PHASE 88 comments to match the surrounding driver style.

The series has two patches: the callback/refactoring patch first, then the
PHASE 88-specific changes.

Testing on the machine with two PHASE 88 racks:

  - Before the changes, scripts/phase88-verify.sh 0 and 1 both passed.
  - After the changes, scripts/phase88-install-module.sh --dry-run built
    cleanly for 6.12.113+deb13-amd64; --reload installed and loaded the
    cleaned module-kit; phase88-verify.sh 1 and 2 both passed (card 0 is now
    the HDMI device).
  - Both PHASE 88 cards had no probe/AC'97 errors or kernel oops, exposed the
    expected multi-track playback/capture devices without consumer PCM devices
    or RFC debug controls, and passed the playback/capture DMA checks. The
    slave card's 44.1 and 96 kHz playback refusals were expected because it
    follows the master clock; the master passed all three rates.

The series applies to the Linux 6.18.58 source and was built and
hardware-tested against the running Debian 6.12.113 kernel. The PHASE 88
MIDI loopback test was not rerun in this pass; the second-UART EEPROM bit is
unchanged.
